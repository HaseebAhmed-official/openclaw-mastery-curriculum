# Instructor Handbook

## Purpose

Enable a qualified teaching team to deliver the program without reconstructing its architecture, while making clear what still requires local execution, calibration, accessibility, security, and policy work.

## Delivery Models

### Full University Program

- prerequisite bridge or equivalent prior course
- two 16-week semesters
- 3 hours instruction/review plus 3 hours supervised lab weekly
- expected 6-10 additional learner hours weekly
- one specialization and capstone board

### One-Semester Foundations Course

- 14-16 weeks
- prerequisite-ready learners
- Semester 1 spine and Phase A/B labs
- minimal-harness practical and delayed transfer
- no production/enterprise claim

### Graduate or Advanced Intensive

- diagnostic test-out of foundations
- 10-14 weeks focused on Semester 2
- requires prior implementation evidence
- production capstone and research/source critique

### Enterprise Adaptation

- role and environment diagnostics
- common security/evaluation/operations gates
- organization-specific tools, policies, incidents, and deployment constraints
- capability decisions rather than academic grades

## Teaching Team

At minimum, the delivery team must cover:

- Python/software design and debugging
- distributed/stateful systems and operations
- security/privacy and authorized lab containment
- LLM/agent architecture and evaluation
- assessment design and learner support

One person may cover multiple areas, but gaps must be documented and addressed through co-instructors, reviewers, or qualified consultation. Product familiarity alone is insufficient.

## Preparation Timeline

### Four to Six Weeks Before Delivery

1. Confirm local outcomes, credits/hours, policies, accommodations, and assessment authority.
2. Run the prerequisite diagnostic and define repair routes.
3. Freeze repository commit and dependency/protocol/framework versions.
4. Execute every required lab in clean target environments.
5. Create unique practical variants and secure hidden fixtures.
6. Review security, privacy, data, network, cost, and acceptable-use boundaries.
7. Calibrate assessors on common anchor work.
8. Verify links and current sources; record deviations.

### One Week Before Delivery

1. Re-run critical lab paths and rollback/cleanup.
2. Test accessible alternatives and offline/deterministic paths.
3. Confirm provider credits or remove network-provider dependency from core assessment.
4. Publish schedule, workload, permitted-agent policy, evidence rules, and support route.
5. Back up frozen teaching and hidden assessment assets separately.

### Every Week

1. Run retrieval and prediction before new explanation.
2. Inspect lab failures and source drift.
3. Return critical feedback before dependent work.
4. Record timing, misconceptions, accessibility issues, and grading ambiguity.
5. Assign repair and later recheck.

## Infrastructure

### Required Core

- Linux, WSL, macOS, or equivalent development environment
- Python 3.11+ and Git
- editor and test runner
- local filesystem/process access appropriate to the labs
- deterministic reference harness; no paid model is required for Semester 1 core

### Required Advanced

- disposable local/container/VM environments
- ability to simulate processes, network failures, queues/state, and rollback
- current MCP/A2A tools or approved fixtures for protocol labs
- trace/metric/log collection appropriate to the selected stack
- controlled environment for authorized attack exercises

### Optional

- cloud deployment
- paid model/provider access
- accelerators/local model serving
- multiple frameworks/products
- external users or enterprise observability systems

Optional resources may enrich evidence but cannot become hidden prerequisites or grade advantages without equitable access.

## Teaching Principles

- Start with stable contracts and deterministic tests.
- Require the simplest valid architecture and a non-agent baseline.
- Teach failures before feature accumulation.
- Pair theory with implementation, evidence, and defense.
- Treat model output, retrieved content, and community claims as untrusted until verified.
- Integrate communication, critical thinking, strategy, problem solving, and technical English into engineering tasks.
- Preserve meaningful human responsibility for tracing, verification, debugging, and final trust.

## Agent-Use and Academic Integrity

Publish permitted use per assessment. Require material assistance disclosure. Use versioned repositories, trace/debug tasks, hidden changes, oral defense, and delayed transfer to assess the learner rather than the artifact.

Do not ban agents from all learning by default; doing so removes a real engineering tool. Do not permit agents to replace designated independent evidence. Fabrication, hidden authorship, or inability to explain work is handled through local integrity policy and mastery gates.

## Safety, Privacy, and High-Stakes Boundaries

- Use synthetic data and disposable targets by default.
- Never require real credentials or unauthorized security testing.
- Define stop, escalation, cleanup, and incident procedures.
- Minimize prompts/logs/traces/submissions containing personal or protected data.
- Require qualified supervision for medical, legal, financial, public-safety, or offensive-security contexts.
- Do not imply certification, regulatory compliance, accreditation, or enterprise readiness without applicable evidence.

## Accessibility

- Provide accessible documents, semantic headings/tables, text alternatives, and captions/transcripts.
- Announce hardware, network, cost, and sensory/motor requirements early.
- Provide equivalent keyboard, screen-reader, low-bandwidth, and deterministic alternatives where feasible.
- Separate English-language support from technical competency; assess communication against the audience and task, not accent.
- Record accommodation decisions without unnecessary sensitive detail.

## Assessment Operations

- Use the Assessment Map and Master Rubric.
- Freeze criteria before reviewing results.
- Secure hidden task variants.
- Calibrate assessors on anchor submissions and record disagreement.
- Moderate critical failures and borderline decisions.
- Preserve concise decision evidence and learner appeal routes.
- Never average away critical safety/authenticity failures.

## Learner Support and Remediation

1. Identify the smallest failed prerequisite or competency.
2. Provide a focused worked example or guided task.
3. Fade support.
4. Retest with changed conditions.
5. Schedule delayed retrieval/transfer.
6. Refer persistent foundation gaps to the bridge rather than adding more advanced content.

## Capstone Management

- Approve scope only when environment, risk, data, compute, and assessor capacity are feasible.
- Require architecture and evidence plan before substantial implementation.
- Conduct security/evaluation gate before deployment.
- Freeze a release candidate for red team and board.
- Assess team artifact and individual mastery separately.
- Reject last-minute feature growth that weakens evidence.

## Continuous Improvement Evidence

Collect, with appropriate privacy:

- diagnostic-to-outcome progression
- lab completion time and failure patterns
- assessment item performance
- assessor agreement and moderation changes
- retry, remediation, and attrition signals
- delayed changed-task transfer
- accessibility/support issues
- source/framework/product drift
- learner and instructor feedback

Do not claim effectiveness from satisfaction or pass rates alone.

## Standalone Delivery Gate

Before calling a release ready to teach, confirm:

- outcomes, modules, labs, and assessments align
- required labs are reproduced from clean instructions
- reference code and fixtures pass
- current sources and case claims are verified
- safety/privacy/accessibility reviews pass
- instructors and assessors are calibrated
- workload and infrastructure are feasible
- learner support and appeal paths exist
- known limitations are published

Before calling it institution-ready, add real cohort and assessor evidence plus independent academic review. Before enterprise-ready, add organizational security, privacy, governance, support, and operational adoption evidence.
