# Weak Submission Red Flags

- “It works” with no reproducible task, test, trace, or end state.
- Framework or product feature lists replace architecture reasoning.
- No deterministic baseline or reason for autonomy.
- No explicit stop, cancellation, budget, or no-progress behavior.
- Tool descriptions are treated as runtime validation or authorization.
- Approval is not bound to exact action/session/arguments.
- Prompt instructions are described as sandboxing.
- Conversation history is described as durable task state.
- Retry is proposed without idempotency, reconciliation, compensation, or external-state reasoning.
- Memory relevance is treated as truth or authority.
- Protocol discovery is treated as trust or permission.
- Security is reduced to prompt injection or a generic checklist.
- Output-only grading ignores actions and end state.
- One trial or public benchmark becomes a production claim.
- Thresholds are changed after results or critical failures are averaged away.
- Logs contain secrets or protected data.
- Product claims are undated, inferred from marketing, or use stale defaults.
- “Multi-tenant,” “enterprise-ready,” “safe,” or “verified” exceeds evidence.
- Agent assistance is hidden or the learner cannot trace submitted work.
- No rollback, recovery, residual risk, or decision owner.

Multiple red flags require deeper authenticity and critical-gate review before any strong grade.
